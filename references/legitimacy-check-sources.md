# Legitimacy check sources

How to check whether the manufacturer/distributor on a label are real, active businesses with a genuine trade relationship — not just whether the product itself has been recalled.

## Customs/trade shipment history

- Search: `"<distributor name>" panjiva` or `"<distributor name>" importgenius` or `cn.panjiva.com <name>`
- What it shows: bill-of-lading records — shipper (manufacturer), consignee (distributor/importer), address, HS codes (product category), and how far back the relationship goes.
- Read: a multi-year pattern of shipments between the named manufacturer and distributor is a strong positive signal. Sparse or no results for a distributor that claims to be an established importer is worth flagging — but note some trade-data sites only index certain ports/manifest types, so absence isn't always conclusive; say so if uncertain.

## FDA Food Facility Registration (food products only)

- Search: `FDA food facility registration <company name>` or check via FDA's public registration search tools when accessible.
- What it shows: whether the manufacturer/importer is registered as required under the Bioterrorism Act / FSMA for facilities that manufacture, process, pack, or hold food for US consumption.
- Read: registration is a baseline regulatory requirement, not a safety certification — but its absence for an importer that should have one is a real flag.

## Business registry lookup

- US: state Secretary of State business search (e.g. `bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov` for California) — confirms entity name, status (active/suspended/dissolved), formation date, registered agent.
- Search generically: `"<company name>" secretary of state business search` or `"<company name>" Dun & Bradstreet`.
- Read: an entity that doesn't show up at all, or shows as dissolved/suspended while still shipping product, is a meaningful red flag.

## Address consistency

- Compare: label address vs. registry address vs. shipping-record address.
- Read: mismatches are common and often benign (separate office/warehouse/mailing addresses) — name the discrepancy but don't over-weight it unless combined with other gaps.

## Home-country legitimacy signals (manufacturer)

- Search: `"<manufacturer name>" news` in the manufacturer's home-language press, plus English-language coverage.
- What to look for: industry rankings, disclosed revenue, state/trade-association recognition, or — the flip side — public scandal, boycotts, government sanctions.
- Read: for a manufacturer of real size, being wholly invisible in home-country business press is itself worth noting.

## Contact-channel professionalism

- Look at: contact email domain (personal webmail vs. company domain), whether a listed phone number/website actually work, whether the website (if any) matches the label's claims.
- Read: treat as a **soft signal only**. Personal webmail is common among small, lean, or family-run importers and isn't disqualifying on its own — weight it against the other checks above rather than as a standalone verdict.

## Weighing it together

Don't let any single weak signal (e.g. a Gmail contact address) carry the verdict. Combine:
- Strong positive: real shipment history + active registration + no adverse news
- Genuine concern: no findable registration + no shipment history + weak contact channel, especially stacked together
- Ambiguous: mixed signals (e.g. real shipment history but personal-email contact and no registry hit) — present as ambiguous rather than forcing a verdict.
